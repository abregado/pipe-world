# Lighting and Day/Night Cycle

How the world is lit, post-processed and recoloured over the day. Read this before changing the sun, ambient, skybox or night grade.

---

## Design

The only light that is always present is a soft-shadow directional `Sun`; the torches add point lights near the start (see [realtime-lights.md](realtime-lights.md)). Everything else is lit by flat ambient colour, so unlit sides never go fully dark. At night the sun is switched off and a colour grade recolours the scene toward plum and amber. **The night palette is deliberately not blue.**

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| URP asset settings | [WorldSetup.cs:35](../Assets/Editor/WorldSetup.cs) (`ConfigureUrpAsset`), applied to `Assets/Settings/PC_RPAsset.asset` |
| Post-processing profile | `Assets/Settings/WorldVolumeProfile.asset`, built in `WorldSetup.BuildVolumeProfile` |
| Night grade | `Assets/Art/LUTs/NightLUT.png`, applied by the [LightGrade pass](realtime-lights.md#light-grade) |
| Sky shader | [GradientSky.shader](../Assets/Shaders/GradientSky.shader) |
| Runtime driver | [DayNightCycle.cs](../Assets/Scripts/World/DayNightCycle.cs) |

## URP Asset

`PC_RPAsset` is configured for one main shadow-casting light: HDR on (64-bit, for the [light mask](realtime-lights.md#light-grade) in alpha), alpha processing on, 4x MSAA, soft shadows, 4 cascades, a 4096 main-light shadow map, 220 m shadow distance. Additional lights render per pixel with a limit of 8 per object, and additional-light shadows use the asset's 4096 atlas ([realtime-lights.md](realtime-lights.md#what-additional-lights-need)).

**Ambient is kept well below the sun so sun shadows read.** By day the ambient is about 0.4 (linear) and the sun peaks at 1.4, adding about 0.4 on flat ground in the morning. A morning shadow is about half the brightness of the sunlit ground beside it before post-processing. The snowy albedo pushes lit ground into the tonemapper's shoulder, so on screen the difference is smaller. At the old values (ambient about 0.7, sun 1.1) it was under 10%. Night ambient is about 0.2, so torch pools stand out against it. `.\shot.ps1 -DebugView 2` shows the sun's shadow attenuation directly, and `-Ambient` scales the ambient for comparison ([screenshots.md](screenshots.md)).

The `ambientColor` gradient and `maxSunIntensity` are serialized in `World.unity`, so changing the code defaults alone does nothing until `-Setup` rebuilds the scene.

## Post-Processing

`WorldVolumeProfile` is a global volume on the `Global Volume` object. Its overrides:

| Effect | Setting |
|---|---|
| Tonemapping | Neutral |
| Bloom | threshold 1.0, intensity 0.25, scatter 0.6 |
| Color Adjustments | contrast +8, saturation +6 |
| Vignette | intensity 0.18, smoothness 0.5 |
| Split Toning | shadows (0.78, 0.78, 0.88), highlights (1.0, 0.95, 0.87) |
| Depth of Field | Gaussian, start 900 m, end 2600 m, max radius 1, so the lowland stays readable and only its far ridges soften |

Screen-space ambient occlusion is switched off; see [visibility-and-depth.md](visibility-and-depth.md#post-processing). Distance fog is applied by the terrain and scatter shaders from values `DayNightCycle` sets; see [visibility-and-depth.md](visibility-and-depth.md#fog).

The camera has post-processing enabled through `UniversalAdditionalCameraData`.

### Night LUT

`NightLUT.png` is a 32x32x32 strip (1024x32), generated by `NightGrade` in [WorldSetup.cs](../Assets/Editor/WorldSetup.cs). Each colour is desaturated by half, tinted from plum (shadows) to amber (highlights) by its luminance, then darkened. The texture is imported with sRGB off, no mipmaps, clamp wrap and no compression; **a LUT imported with sRGB on or compression enabled shifts every colour in the grade.**

The volume has no Color Lookup override. The [LightGrade pass](realtime-lights.md#light-grade) applies this LUT after post-processing, weighted by the night factor, so that nearby lights can replace it locally.

## Time of Day

[DayNightCycle.cs:100](../Assets/Scripts/World/DayNightCycle.cs) (`Apply`) runs every frame and in edit mode (`[ExecuteAlways]`).

`timeOfDay` runs 0 to 1: 0 is midnight, 0.25 sunrise, 0.5 noon, 0.75 sunset. `dayLengthSeconds` (default 600) sets how fast it advances; `paused` freezes it.

From `timeOfDay`, `Apply` derives the sun elevation (-1 at midnight, +1 at noon), then:

| Output | Derived from |
|---|---|
| Sun rotation | `timeOfDay * 360 - 90` pitch, `sunHeading` yaw |
| Sun colour and intensity | `sunColor` gradient and a fast ramp after elevation passes 0 |
| Sun enabled | elevation above 0.01 |
| Ambient colour | `ambientColor` gradient, flat ambient mode |
| Sky | `skyZenith`, `skyHorizon` gradients; ground colour is 90% of horizon |
| Night factor | `InverseLerp(nightStart, nightFull, elevation)`, written to the `_PW_NightFactor` shader global |

**The sun is disabled below the horizon.** A directional light below the horizon points upward and would cast shadows from underneath the ground.

The gradients are sampled by elevation remapped to 0..1 (0 midnight, 0.5 horizon, 1 noon), so stops near 0.5 control sunrise and sunset colours.

`DayNightCycle` instantiates its own copy of the sky material at runtime, so edits to the skybox never modify the asset.

## Sky Shader

[GradientSky.shader](../Assets/Shaders/GradientSky.shader) blends horizon to zenith above the horizon and horizon to ground below it. The blend is quantised into `_Bands` (10) equal steps, the ground blend into three, and the sun's glow into five rings, so the sky has the same hard-edged, stepped look as the faceted ground.

**The bands are widest at sunrise and sunset and squeezed toward the horizon at noon and midnight.** `DayNightCycle` sets `_BandSqueeze` to the sun elevation's magnitude to the power 1.5, and the shader divides the horizon falloff exponent by up to 16 (`exp2(-4 * _BandSqueeze)`). At noon and midnight the zenith colour fills almost the whole sky, and the other nine bands fit in the lowest few degrees, mostly behind the crater walls. A sun disc and glow are drawn toward `_SunDir`, which `DayNightCycle` sets from the sun transform. The disc is HDR, so bloom picks it up, and is masked to the sky above the horizon.
